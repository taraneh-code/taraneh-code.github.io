Elite Advisor — New Role Training

To equip a team for a newly merged role with confidence, not confusion.

Created for a group of sales and support team members, this training introduced a brand-new role that combined the functions and skills of two previously separate positions. The challenge wasn't just teaching new tasks. It was helping people reframe their whole sense of the job.

Read the story

The problem

High-end buyers who requested a first home showing got templated confirmations and often didn't speak to a person until the showing itself. More than half of those first showings were canceled.

What I did

I built the training for a pilot team in a brand-new role, the Elite Advisor, which combined the work of two separate positions: showing coordinator and sales advisor. I delivered it live and in person to the pilot group.

How I designed it

  • Identity before tasks. The role is built around five named strengths (Tour Titan, Master of Communication, Elevated Experience Orchestrator, Connoisseur, and Seamless Customer Concierge), so people learn who they're becoming before what they're doing.
  • Discovery before instruction. Early on, I ask the group what sets the Ritz-Carlton apart from the Holiday Inn when both offer a room and a bed. They work out what "white glove" means for themselves.
  • Practice, not just explanation. Participants write their own opening and say it aloud, then role-play rapport-building with customer scripts, then practice writing the handoff to the agent from templates.
  • Clear contrast with the old jobs. Two "what's different" sections show showing coordinators and sales advisors exactly what changes for each. For example, the new role cross-sells but doesn't schedule.
  • The real workflow, in the real tools. A step-by-step walkthrough runs from the queue through pre-contact review, outreach, booking, and warm handoff, with a hands-on demo and practice.

The result

I delivered the training live to the pilot group. It shows how I turn a role change into training that starts with identity and ends with practice in the real tools.

Training Proposal Sales Onboarding

New Rep Sales Training Proposal

To pitch a structured onboarding path for new sales reps, not just a syllabus.

A proposal outlining a training approach for ramping new sales representatives, covering what they need to learn, in what order, and why, with an eye toward getting reps to full productivity faster and with more confidence.

Read the story

The problem

A national B2B sales organization needed new reps to ramp faster and get core skills in place early, without hurting performance or job satisfaction. (Confidential: the organization is kept anonymous.)

What I did

I wrote the plan I would use to build a national sales enablement onboarding and strategy: a structured 90-day path with clear checkpoints, owners, and goals.

How I designed it

  • A simple frame. The program is organized around four Cs: Compliance, Clarification, Culture, and Connection. That keeps what reps learn clear and the sequence easy for managers to follow.
  • Mostly on the job. The first two weeks split roughly half instructor-led and half self-paced, with e-learning, call listening, and CRM sandbox practice. After that, learning moves into the role and support tapers over time.
  • Support from three people. Each rep works with a manager, a peer mentor, and a trainer through weekly 1:1s, coaching sessions, and office hours. Shadowing runs both ways: reps shadow peers, then peers shadow the rep.
  • Three stages with clear targets. Absorb (30 days), Practice (60 days, at 75% of target), and Internalize (90 days, meeting conversion goals).
  • Visible progress. Progress reports at Week 1, Week 2, and Days 30, 60, and 90 show managers problems early.

The result

This is a proposal, so there are no results to report. It shows how I plan and structure enablement at scale: how to build it, how to support it, and how to measure it.
